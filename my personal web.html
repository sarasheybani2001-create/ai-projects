01 / The person behind the screen

A little curiosity. A lot of questions.

I've been naturally curious for as long as I can remember. I was probably one of those children who couldn't accept an explanation without asking five more questions. Apparently, I never grew out of that. Sorry to everyone involved.

Over time, that curiosity developed into an interest in logic, mathematics, technology, and human behavior. I like understanding how things work, connecting seemingly unrelated ideas, and occasionally questioning things that everyone else has already agreed to accept.

Artificial intelligence caught my attention because it combines several things I genuinely enjoy: analytical thinking, creativity, problem-solving, and a rather interesting opportunity to build things that actually matter.

I'm still learning, experimenting, and figuring out where I want to take all of this. I don't have everything figured out. Honestly, that would make things a little boring.

"The interesting questions are usually the ones nobody bothered to ask."

Where I've been. Where I'm going.

The beginning

Asking too many questions.

A natural habit of questioning explanations, looking for patterns, and trying to understand how things work. It eventually became more than just an annoying personality trait.

Academic development

Mathematics found its way in.

Developing an interest in mathematical thinking, logic, and problem-solving. The more I learned, the more I noticed how many connections existed between different fields.

From understanding to building.

Working toward stronger technical skills, exploring programming and artificial intelligence, and turning ideas into actual projects instead of leaving them as interesting thoughts.

My ambition is to become an engineer who develops intelligent systems that solve real problems, particularly in education and human learning. Something useful, not just something that looks impressive in a presentation.
